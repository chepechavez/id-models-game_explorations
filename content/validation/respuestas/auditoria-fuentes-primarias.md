## 1. Action Mapping — Cathy Moore (2017), *Map It: The Hands-On Guide to Strategic Training Design*

- **Casos validados:** `am-1` (*La caída en la recuperación de cartera vencida*), `am-2` (*Fuerza comercial y los primeros 45 segundos de contacto*), `am-3` (*Fugas de datos por suplantación telefónica en banca digital*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **Alcance corporativo vs. académico (Intro, p. 5):**
     > *"Keep in mind that this book describes training created for businesses and other organizations that want to improve their performance. This isn’t an academic book, and it doesn’t talk about educating children or university students. If you’re an educator, you might find some useful ideas here, but you won’t be able to apply the model as it’s intended."*
  2. **Conducta observable vs. información (Cap. 2, p. 30):**
     > *"In the vast majority of training projects, we need to help people do something, not just know about something. Even when our goal is ostensibly to 'raise awareness,' we actually need people to behave differently as a result of their increased awareness."*
  3. **Los pasos de Action Mapping (Intro, pp. 7–8):**
     > *"1. Write the goal for your project [...] 2. Ask, 'What do they need to do, and why aren’t they doing it?' [...] 3. Brainstorm practice activities [...] 4. Include only the must-have information [...] 5. Create a stream of activities, not a presentation."*
  4. **El análisis causal en el Paso 2 y las soluciones no formativas (Cap. 6, p. 113 & p. 122):**
     > *"Why aren’t they doing it? [...] You identify what makes each action hard to do, and you propose solutions connected to the causes of the problem. Those solutions might NOT include training. This is the most important step. Without this step, it isn’t action mapping."*
     > *"If people can follow the job aid without scratching their heads or making costly errors, they don’t need training... Just make sure the job aid is available where and when they need it."*
  5. **El artefacto de salida (Cap. 14, p. 358):**
     > *"Do you really have to write a conventional design document? No! You already have a detailed map that shows the justification for every element of the training, and you have a prototype that demonstrates how the elements work together... Here’s an example of a four-page project outline that hits the highlights, links to the action map and prototype, and lays out the responsibilities and schedule."*

## 2. 4C-ID — van Merriënboer, Clark & de Croock (2002); van Merriënboer (2019/2021); van Merriënboer, Kirschner & Frèrejean (2024), *Ten Steps to Complex Learning* (4th ed.)

- **Casos validados:** `4cid-1` (*Colapso de analistas novatos en centro de respuesta a ciberataques*), `4cid-2` (*Médicos residentes ante crisis hemodinámica en urgencias*), `4cid-3` (*Residencia en cirugía laparoscópica mínimamente invasiva*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **Cuándo prescribir y cuándo NO prescribir 4C-ID (2024, Cap. 1, p. 11):**
     > *"We recommend the Ten Steps when your instructional objective is to develop one or more complex skills requiring integrating knowledge, skills, and attitudes and coordinating constituent skills. Such skills involve substantial amounts of problem solving, reasoning, or decision making... In contrast, the Ten Steps approach can be excessive for tasks involving little problem solving, reasoning, and/or decision making; for example, for teaching procedural tasks such as stocking shelves or calibrating a machine... A rule-of-thumb is that complex skill development in programs shorter than 100 hours is unlikely."*
  2. **Enfoque holístico de tareas completas vs. fragmentación atomista (2019, p. 15 / 2021, p. 15):**
     > *"Es un enfoque de tareas completas en el cual el núcleo del programa de entrenamiento consiste en tareas basadas en la vida real que abordan la habilidad compleja completa. Esto contrasta con los enfoques atomistas donde los contenidos y las habilidades complejas se reducen a elementos simples."*
  3. **Los 4 Componentes del Training Blueprint (2024, Cap. 2, pp. 16–17):**
     > *"1. Learning tasks: authentic whole-task experiences based on real-life tasks and situations integrating knowledge, skills, and attitudes. The whole set of learning tasks exhibits high variability, is organized in simple-to-complex task classes, and exhibits diminishing learner support and guidance within each task class (i.e., scaffolding). 2. Supportive information... 3. Procedural information... best presented just in time... 4. Part-task practice: practice items provided to help learners reach a very high level of automaticity for selected routine aspects of a task... but only starts after the routine aspect has been introduced in the context of a whole, meaningful learning task."*
  4. **Regulación de la carga cognitiva entre Información de Apoyo e Información Procedimental (2024, Cap. 2, Box 2.1, pp. 28–29):**
     > *"Because supportive information typically has high element interactivity, it is preferable not to present it to learners while working on the learning tasks. Simultaneously performing a task and studying the information would almost certainly cause cognitive overload... Procedural information consists of step-by-step instructions and corrective feedback and typically has much lower element interactivity... therefore, procedural information is preferably presented precisely when learners need it."*

## 3. ADDIE — Robert M. Branch (2009), *Instructional Design: The ADDIE Approach*

- **Casos validados:** `addie-1` (*Coordinación multiproveedor para virtualizar 15 escuelas corporativas*), `addie-2` (*Fábrica institucional para digitalizar 80 asignaturas en 10 sedes*), `addie-3` (*Modernización nacional de brigadas de emergencia con validación de brechas*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **ADDIE como paradigma de desarrollo de productos y término paraguas (Prólogo, pp. 5, 18, 20):**
     > *"ADDIE is a product development paradigm and not a model per se... 'It is not a specific, fully elaborated model in its own right, but rather an umbrella term that refers to a family of models that share a common underlying structure' (Molenda, 2003, page 35)."*
     > *"There is no single correct set of ADDIE procedures or no single correct sequence of ADDIE procedures."*
  2. **Filtro de causas de discrepancia de desempeño (Prólogo, pp. 12–14):**
     > *"There are three general categories of reasons why a performance discrepancy exists: (1) limited resources, (2) lack of motivation, and (3) lack of knowledge and skill... Thus, the purpose of instruction is to close the gap in performance when it is caused by a lack of knowledge or skill. Alternatives to instruction should be considered when a performance gap is primarily caused by lack of resources or lack of motivation."*
  3. **Los 5 entregables canónicos por fase (Prólogo, pp. 17–18 & Fig. 2, p. 3):**
     > *"The typical deliverable for the Analyze phase is an Analysis Summary... The typical deliverable for the Design phase is a Design Brief [conduct a task inventory, compose performance objectives, generate testing strategies, and calculate return on investment]... The typical deliverable for the Develop phase are all of the Learning Resources... The typical deliverable for the Implement phase is an Implementation Strategy... The typical deliverable for the Evaluate phase is an Evaluation Plan."*

## 4. Enfoque de Sistemas — Dick, Carey & Carey (2015), *The Systematic Design of Instruction* (8th ed.)

- **Casos validados:** `dc-1` (*Maniobra crítica de desenergización en subestación de alta tensión*), `dc-2` (*Certificación nacional replicable para técnicos de calibración aeronáutica*), `dc-3` (*Operación de radar de alerta temprana con diagnóstico de fallas por subhabilidad*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **Raíz cognitivista (Gagné) y apertura constructivista (Cap. 1, pp. 3–4):**
     > *"Our original approach to this component of the model was heavily influenced by the work of Robert Gagné’s The Conditions of Learning (1965), which incorporated cognitive information-processing views of learning... Throughout this text, readers will find predominately a cognitivist view of teaching and learning, but will also see elements of constructivist thinking adapted as appropriate."*
  2. **Alta pertinencia industrial/corporativa/militar y límite en la docencia diaria (Cap. 1, pp. 9–10):**
     > *"The third and perhaps most important reason for using the systems approach is that it is an empirical and replicable process... Although widely adopted among educators at all levels, the systems approach finds even more numerous applications in business and industry, government, nonprofits, nongovernmental organizations (NGOs), and the military."*
     > *"The individual instructor with day-to-day instructional responsibilities can use the complete process to develop only small amounts of instruction at any given time because of the level of detail included in each step."*
  3. **Análisis jerárquico, Design Evaluation Chart y diagnóstico por subhabilidad (Cap. 4, p. 67; Cap. 7, pp. 140, 155):**
     > *"The whole point of using the hierarchical approach is to identify just what the learner must know to be successful—nothing more, and nothing less."*
     > *"If a student fails to perform the terminal objective, the designer should be able to identify where in the learning process the student began not to understand the instruction. By examining whether each item is answered correctly and linking the correct and incorrect responses to the anchor subordinate skill, the designer should be able to do exactly that."*

## 5. Pebble-in-the-Pond — M. David Merrill (2002, 2008, 2013, 2017), *First Principles of Instruction*

- **Casos validados:** `pitp-1` (*El programa de desarrollo web donde nadie programa hasta el segundo mes*), `pitp-2` (*Programa de resiliencia industrial para jefes de planta*), `pitp-3` (*Especialización en contención de brotes epidemiológicos de complejidad creciente*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **Instrucción centrada en problemas vs. centrada en temas (2002, pp. 44–45):**
     > *"Problem-centered instruction is contrasted with topic-centered instruction where components of the task are taught in isolation (e.g., 'You won’t understand this now but later it will really be important to you') before introducing the real world task to the students."*
  2. **Las 6 ondas exactas de Pebble-in-the-Pond (2013, Cap. 11, p. 252):**
     > *"The instructional products comprising the first ripple are a prototype demonstration and application of this problem. The second ripple is a progression of problems of the same type... The third ripple is the component skills required to solve this class of problems... The fourth ripple is enhance strategies and consists of structural framework and peer-interaction... The fifth ripple is finalize design... The sixth ripple is evaluation and contains data collection, formative evaluation, and prototype revision."*
  3. **Enfoque Content-First, Prototipo Funcional y eliminación de errores de traducción (2013, Cap. 11, pp. 269–270):**
     > *"The Pebble approach compresses steps in the ISD process by moving directly to the development of a functional prototype of the actual instructional strategy, subsuming goal analysis, performance objectives, and media selection into a combined instructional design activity... Typically, this [traditional ISD] process produces an instructional design document. A significant problem with this approach is the number of translation errors that can occur when developing the actual instruction from the abstract design document."*
  4. **Límites explícitos del modelo (2013, Cap. 11, pp. 252–253):**
     > *"The pond of the Pebble model assumes that some front-end analysis has already determined that there is a problem that can be solved by instruction rather than by some other means... The Pebble model is a design rather than a development model. This means that the important steps of production, implementation, and summative evaluation are not considered in this book."*

## 6. Backward Design / UbD — Grant Wiggins & Jay McTighe (2005, 2011, 2012), *Understanding by Design*

- **Casos validados:** `ubd-1` (*Calificaciones perfectas en cuestionarios, cero criterio en ensayos históricos*), `ubd-2` (*Talleres divertidos en ingeniería de software pero arquitecturas que colapsan*), `ubd-3` (*Dilemas bioéticos reales en el internado médico frente a exámenes de fechas*).
- **Citas textuales verificadas:**
  1. **Los Pecados Gemelos del diseño tradicional (2005, Cap. 1, pp. 16, 20):**
     > *"We call these the 'twin sins' of traditional design. The error of activity-oriented design might be called 'hands-on without being minds-on'—engaging experiences that lead only accidentally, if at all, to insight or achievement... A second form of aimlessness goes by the name of 'coverage,' an approach in which students march through a textbook, page by page... This approach might be termed 'teaching by mentioning it.'"*
  2. **Pensar como un evaluador y la analogía judicial (2005, Cap. 7, p. 148):**
     > *"The mantra of this and the next chapter is to think like an assessor, not a teacher... We urge teachers to consider a judicial analogy as they plan assessment. Think of students as juries think of the accused: innocent (of understanding, skill, and so on) until proven guilty by a preponderance of evidence that is more than circumstantial."*
  3. **Las 6 Facetas de la Comprensión y la unidad como foco mínimo (2012, pp. 1, 7):**
     > *"Six facets of understanding—the capacity to explain, interpret, apply, shift perspective, empathize, and self-assess—can serve as indicators of understanding."*
     > *"However, we do not recommend isolated lesson planning separate from unit planning. We have chosen the unit as a focus for design because the key elements of the UbD framework—understandings, essential questions, and transfer performance tasks—are too complex and multifaceted to be satisfactorily addressed within a single lesson."*
